Why now

Three trends converge in a 12–18 month window

Sequencing cost has collapsed to the point where genomic data now outpaces clinics' ability to interpret it. Germany's DiGA pathway reimburses digital health tools directly through statutory insurance — 73M lives, 170K prescribing physicians, ~€171M paid across ~700K prescriptions in 2025. And the EU AI Act's high-risk classification for clinical AI takes effect August 2026, raising the compliance bar in a way that rewards early, compliant-by-design entrants over late arrivals.

The problem & solution

Genomic data is outrunning the clinic's ability to read it

Problem

Genomic data is growing exponentially, but clinics and healthcare providers lack secure, fast, user-friendly tools to turn it into actionable, personalized treatment decisions.

Solution

Genifeld is a secure, AI-driven cloud platform that transforms complex sequencing data into precise clinical insights — starting with pharmacogenomics, the clinical use case with the fastest path to routine physician adoption and the clearest DiGA reimbursement fit.

Competitive moat

The edge isn't “better AI”

SOPHiA GENETICS, Tempus, Genoox and PathAI are lab- and hospital-enterprise-facing, US/CH-headquartered, and largely oncology-focused. None are structured around German reimbursement, and none compete head-on in pharmacogenomics. Matching Genifeld requires a competitor to run a comparable 12+ month BfArM process from scratch, in a clinical niche the large players have largely ignored.
